# CDBurnerXP - Burn Files You Already Have

At a glance:
- A data disc of files you may write
- Audio only from tracks you may copy
- An image of a folder you assembled
- A verify pass before you trust the disc

## Files You Assembled

CDBurnerXP writes a disc or an image from a compilation you build in the window. Add files from a folder you own. Read the list. A wrong file in the list will be on the disc. Remove it before you burn. The source files stay on the hard disk. Burning does not delete them.

Leave enough free space on the PC for a temporary image if the program builds one. A nearly full system disk is a bad place to start. Label the disc with a name you will understand later. The label is not a backup catalog by itself. Keep the folder you burned from.

## Audio You May Copy

An audio compilation is for tracks you own: a disc you were allowed to rip, or files you bought. Do not fill a CD with music you do not have rights to copy. If a track will not add because it is protected, stop. Do not look for a bypass. Gap and order are choices you see before the burn. Listen to the first track from the finished disc.

## Image Of Your Folder

Creating an ISO of a folder you assembled is a way to keep that set as one file. It is not a copy of a protected movie disc. Mount or open the ISO and check that the files you added are the files inside. Then you may burn that image to a blank disc you own. Do not treat a downloaded commercial ISO as something this page helps you get.

## Verify Before You File The Disc

After the burn, verify if the program offers it, and open a file from the disc. A burn that reported success can still have a disc the drive will not read next week. If verify fails, do not file that disc as the only copy. Burn another blank from the folder that is still on the hard disk.

## Blank Media

Use a blank disc you intend to write. Do not experiment on the only disc of a family recording until you have succeeded with a test folder. Eject and read the disc in the same drive or another drive you have. The hard-disk folder remains the master until that read works.

## What This Is Not

This is not a video ripping bench and not a tool for game discs that refuse a copy. Data you created, audio you may use, and images of those sets are the scope. A codec or a crack is not part of setup. If an installer offers extra software you did not come for, decline it.

## Help Desk

Verify failed? Do not archive that disc as the spare. Burn another from the folder on disk.
A track will not add? It may be protected. Stop.
The disc is empty in another drive? The burn did not finish. Check the log and keep the hard-disk files.
You added the wrong folder? Remove it before you burn. The list is the disc.
An extra program appeared in the installer? Decline it. You came for the burner.

## Reader Notes

A small test folder is the right first disc. The list on screen is what will be written. Read it. The hard disk copy stays until a drive has opened the disc.

Audio and data are different compilations. Do not mix a hope of copying a commercial DVD into a data project. If the disc is locked, it is out of scope.

Label the disc and keep the folder name in agreement. Next year the sticker is not as clear as the directory you still have on disk.
